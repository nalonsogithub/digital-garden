## The question this track follows

Every useful computation depends on structure the problem already has. The three essays here are about three different places that structure can come from, and about what happens at the edge where it runs out.

**Derived.** [[from-smooth-to-jagged|From Smooth to Jagged]] follows mathematics growing a new tool each time reality proved rougher than the last one could describe: Taylor series, Fourier analysis, Itô calculus, fractal geometry, rough paths. Structure is derived from first principles, and the frontier is still moving.

**Borrowed.** [[the-resolution-of-reality|The Resolution of Reality]] is a primer on quantum computing, and its central claim is that a quantum computer's advantage depends on how native the problem is to the machine. At the native end, the machine and the molecule obey the same physics and the modeling step nearly disappears. Structure is borrowed from nature.

**Learned.** [[from-prediction-to-agency|From Prediction to Agency]], on frontier language models, follows a different arc: each stage in their development answered a limit hit by the previous one, and the models now learn structure from data where nobody can derive it and nothing in nature computes it. It ends with the question the first two essays set up: whether a learned model of human behavior could ever become a borrowed substrate for markets, and why [[reflexivity]] puts a ceiling on that hope.
